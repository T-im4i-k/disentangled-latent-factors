\begin{abstract}
  Email outreach enables direct communication with customers, but contacting
  every recipient with every campaign is costly and may be perceived as spam.
  Recently, \v{Z}id et al.\ proposed to select the recipients of a new campaign
  in batches by means of a multi-armed bandit (MAB) with Thompson Sampling (TS),
  whose parameters are derived from a shallow autoencoder (SAE) trained on
  historical recipient--template opens. This framework is purely collaborative:
  its scores are derived from interactions alone, and each campaign template is
  an opaque identifier, although the decision to open a message depends not only
  on collaborative patterns but also on the content of the message. In this
  work, we investigate how dense embeddings of template texts can supply this
  item signal to the SAE-based MAB by exploiting the similarity between
  templates, while retaining its main properties: no retraining during active
  learning and interpretable components. The original SAE-based MAB serves as
  the baseline.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{Email Outreach, Cold-Start Recommendation, Multi-Armed Bandits,

\section{Introduction}

Email outreach remains an important marketing channel, yet contacting all
recipients with every campaign is costly and, when messages are irrelevant,
damages the sender's reputation~\cite{zid2025active}. This motivates
recommending each campaign only to the recipients most likely to open it. Every
new campaign is a cold-start item, but under a sending budget and a deadline,
messages can be delivered in batches, and the opens observed after each batch
guide the next selection. This active learning setting combines collaborative
filtering (CF) with multi-armed bandits (MAB)~\cite{zhao2013interactive}, with
Thompson Sampling (TS) as a simple and effective
policy~\cite{russo2018tutorial}.

\v{Z}id et al.~\cite{zid2025active} solve it with a MAB whose arms are
recipients and whose TS parameters are derived from a shallow autoencoder
(SAE)~\cite{steck2019embarrassingly} trained offline on historical opens, so no
retraining is needed during the campaign. On 131,918 recipients and 160
templates, it reaches a recall of 0.923 when contacting 25\% of the recipients
and outperforms a range of baselines across most sending fractions. We refer to
this model as the \emph{SAE-based MAB} (recapped in
Section~\ref{sec:recap}) and use it as our baseline.

The SAE-based MAB is purely collaborative: all its signals are derived from the
interaction matrix, and a template is only a row index of it. Yet whether a
recipient opens a message depends not only on the CF signal, i.e., on patterns
shared by recipients with similar behavior, but also on the item signal, i.e.,
on the content of the message, e.g., a promotional offer versus a newsletter.
Without the item signal, (1)~the CF signal for a new template is absent at the
start of a campaign, so the scores are template-independent and the first batch
is the same for any new template, and (2)~the influence between recipients
learned by the autoencoder is shared by all templates.

The item signal is available exactly where the CF signal is not: the text of a
template is known before the first message is sent and can be encoded into a
dense vector by a pretrained language model~\cite{reimers2019sentence}, and
similarities between embeddings identify past campaigns that are informative
about the new one. Complementing the CF signal with item content is the idea of
hybrid recommenders, which alleviate
cold-start~\cite{wang2011collaborative,volkovs2017dropoutnet}, but these methods
learn content-to-latent mappings on large catalogs, whereas here only 160 templates
are available, retraining within a running campaign is undesirable, and
interpretability is desired. The closest works use contextual bandits to select
promotional offers in email campaigns~\cite{kocherzhenko2022evaluating} or
structured attributes for active learning under item
cold-start~\cite{zhu2019addressing}; to the best of our knowledge, neither uses
the free text of campaigns in a CF-based recipient selection framework.
% TODO: verify this claim with a more thorough literature search.
We therefore investigate how template text embeddings can be incorporated into
the SAE-based MAB.

\section{Recap of the Original Model}\label{sec:recap}

\paragraph{Setup.} Let $X \in \{0,1\}^{n \times m}$ be the interaction matrix of
$n$ historical templates and $m$ recipients, where $X_{ij} = 1$ if recipient $j$
opened template $i$. A new template $n+1$ is sent within a deadline $T$ in $b$
batches of $k \ll m$ recipients: the first batch at $t = 0$, the following ones
at intervals of $T/b$, and a final batch at $T$. Let $x(t) \in \{0,1\}^m$ mark
the recipients who have opened template $n+1$ in $[0,t)$, so that $x(0) = \mathbf{0}$,
and let $\bar{x}(t) = x(t) / \max(1, \mathbf{1}^{\top} x(t))$. The goal is to
reach as many of the eventual openers as possible within the sending budget.

\paragraph{Shallow autoencoder.} An SAE with encoder and decoder
$E, D \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d}$ is trained once on $X$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sae}
  \min_{E,D}\; \ell\bigl(X,\, \sigma(X B_{E,D})\bigr), \qquad
  B_{E,D} = E D^{\top} - \operatorname{diag}\bigl([E \odot D]\,\mathbf{1}_d\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\ell$ is the element-wise binary cross-entropy, $\sigma$ the sigmoid, and
the diagonal term sets the diagonal of $B_{E,D}$ to zero, ruling out trivial
self-prediction. The entry $\Sigma_{ij}$ of $\Sigma = \sigma(B_{E,D})$ is the
probability that recipient $j$ opens a template given that only recipient $i$
opened it, i.e., the influence of $i$ on $j$.

\paragraph{Arm score.} Each recipient $j$ is an arm with the score
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score}
  s_j(t) = \alpha\, \varphi_j\, p_j + (1-\alpha)\, f_j(t),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:components}
  \begin{aligned}
    \varphi_j &= \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_{ij}, \qquad
    p_j = \frac{1}{m-1}\sum_{i \neq j} \Sigma_{ji}, \\
    f_j(t) &= \bar{x}(t)^{\top} \Sigma_{:,j},
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\varphi_j$ is the historic open rate of recipient $j$, $p_j$ their general
influence on the other recipients, $f_j(t)$ the confidence that $j$ opens given
the opens of template $n+1$ observed so far, and $\alpha \in [0,1]$ trades off the
two terms. All three quantities are derived from interactions alone, so the
score is purely collaborative; the new template enters it only through the
observed opens $\bar{x}(t)$.

\paragraph{Thompson Sampling.} The score parametrizes a Beta distribution for each
recipient,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ts}
  a_j(t) = G\, s_j(t), \qquad b_j(t) = G\,\bigl(1 - s_j(t)\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $G > 0$ is a confidence modifier: the larger $G$, the narrower the
distribution. In each batch, one value is drawn from every distribution and the
$k$ not yet contacted recipients with the highest draws receive the message.
Since $\Sigma$ is fixed after training, no retraining is needed during active
learning.
