\title{Guided Disentangled Latent Factors for Interpretable Active Recipient Selection in Email Outreach}

\author{Tymofii Ivanov}
\email{ivanotym@fit.cvut.cz}
\affiliation{%
  \institution{Faculty of Information Technology, Czech Technical University in Prague}
  \city{Prague}
  \country{Czech Republic}
}

\begin{abstract}
  Email outreach enables direct communication with customers, but contacting
  every recipient with every campaign is costly and may be perceived as spam.
  Recently, \v{Z}id et al.\ proposed to select the recipients of a new campaign
  in batches using a multi-armed bandit (MAB) with Thompson Sampling (TS), whose
  parameters are derived from a shallow autoencoder (SAE) trained on historical
  recipient--template opens. While the components of this framework are
  interpretable at the level of recipients, the latent space of its autoencoder
  is not: nothing makes the latent factors individually meaningful, so the
  representation of a campaign template says little about what kind of campaign
  it is. In this work, we investigate how disentangled latent factors can make
  these template representations semantically meaningful. We combine Euler
  encoding, a non-probabilistic disentanglement technique for autoencoders,
  with latent factor guidance, which steers selected factors towards
  template-level statistics derived from the interaction data, and explore the
  move from a shallow to a deep autoencoder that this requires. Our goal is to
  improve the interpretability of the SAE-based MAB and, if possible, its
  retrieval performance, while preserving its key property of no retraining
  during active learning. The original SAE-based MAB serves as the baseline.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{Email Outreach, Cold-Start Recommendation, Multi-Armed Bandits,
Autoencoders, Disentangled Representations, Interpretability}

\section{Introduction}

Email outreach remains an important marketing channel, yet contacting all
recipients with every campaign is costly and, when messages are irrelevant,
damages the sender's reputation~\cite{zid2025active}. This motivates
recommending each campaign only to the recipients most likely to open it. Every
new campaign is a cold-start item. However, under a sending budget and a
deadline, messages can be delivered in batches, and the opens observed after
each batch guide the next selection. This active learning setting lies at the
intersection of collaborative filtering (CF) and multi-armed bandits
(MAB)~\cite{zhao2013interactive}, where Thompson Sampling (TS) is a simple and
effective policy~\cite{russo2018tutorial}.

\v{Z}id et al.~\cite{zid2025active} address this setting with a MAB in which
each arm is a recipient and the TS parameters are derived from a shallow
autoencoder (SAE)~\cite{steck2019embarrassingly} trained offline on historical
opens, so no retraining is needed during the campaign. On a dataset of 131,918
recipients and 160 templates, it reaches a recall of 0.923 when contacting 25\%
of the recipients and outperforms a range of baselines across most sending
fractions. We refer to this model as the \emph{SAE-based MAB} (recapped in
Section~\ref{sec:recap}) and use it as our baseline.

The SAE-based MAB is interpretable at the level of recipients: the influence
matrix $\Sigma$ states how strongly an open by one recipient signals an open by
another, and each component of the score has a clear meaning. Campaign
templates, however, are only rows of the interaction matrix, and the latent
space through which the autoencoder compresses them has no semantic structure.
The objective in Eq.~\eqref{eq:sae} depends on the encoder and decoder only
through their product $ED^{\top}$, so the latent axes are arbitrary: any
invertible linear transformation of the latent space yields the same model.
Consequently, the latent representation of a template does not tell whether the
template behaves like a promotional offer or a newsletter, how quickly it is
usually opened, or how widely it appeals, and a marketer cannot relate the
recipients selected for a campaign to the characteristics of the campaign. It is
also open whether a more structured template representation can improve the
selected batches, for example by letting the influence between recipients
depend on what kind of campaign is being sent.

Disentangled representation learning aims at latent spaces in which each
dimension captures a single, independent factor of variation in the
data~\cite{bengio2013representation,higgins2018towards}, which makes the
representation easier to interpret. Most approaches build on variational
autoencoders~\cite{kingma2014auto} and regularize the latent
space~\cite{higgins2017beta,kim2018disentangling,chen2018isolating}, which
requires balancing the regularizer against the reconstruction loss and assuming
a prior. The disentangling autoencoder (DAE) of Cha and
Thiyagalingam~\cite{cha2023orthogonality} instead uses a deterministic
autoencoder with Euler encoding, a mapping of each latent variable to its cosine
and sine motivated by the symmetry-based definition of
disentanglement~\cite{higgins2018towards}, and needs no regularizer. Still,
disentanglement without any inductive bias or supervision is not
identifiable~\cite{locatello2019challenging}, so nothing guarantees that the
learned factors are those a practitioner cares about, while even few labels can
be effective~\cite{locatello2019disentangling}. CODAE~\cite{cha2025discovering}
addresses this with latent factor guidance: an additional loss steers designated
latent factors towards known quantities computed from the input itself (image
moments) with a weight that decreases to zero during training, so the model can
refine them, while Euler encoding disentangles the remaining factors. In
recommendation, disentanglement has been used to separate user intents or item
concepts~\cite{ma2019learning,wang2020disentangled}; a recent study finds
that it correlates with the interpretability of the learned representation, but
this does not necessarily carry over to the interpretability of the model's
output~\cite{dervishaj2025are}.

These ideas suit the SAE-based MAB for two reasons. First, guidance targets can
be computed from the interaction matrix alone, e.g., the open rate or time-to-open
statistics of each historical template, so no side information about recipients
or messages is needed, which is important given the data protection constraints
of email outreach~\cite{zid2025active}. Second, the latent factors describe
templates, which is exactly the level at which the original model lacks
semantics. To the best of our knowledge, guided disentangled latent factors
have not been used in a CF-based recipient selection framework.
% TODO: verify this claim with a more thorough literature search.
Realizing them, however, changes the model: Euler encoding is a nonlinear layer,
so the shallow autoencoder has to give way to a deep one, as in deep CF
autoencoders~\cite{liang2018variational}, and the influence matrix $\Sigma$ is
then no longer a closed-form product but has to be obtained from the network.

We therefore investigate how guided disentangled latent factors can be
incorporated into the SAE-based MAB while preserving its main properties: no
retraining during active learning and interpretable components. Specifically, we
(1)~replace the shallow autoencoder with a deep one whose latent space is
passed through Euler encoding, (2)~guide a subset of latent factors towards
template-level statistics derived from the interactions, and
(3)~evaluate the result in two ways: disentanglement on a semi-synthetic dataset
with known template factors using supervised metrics~\cite{carbonneau2022measuring},
and, on real data, the relation of the factors to template statistics together
with the recall of the selected recipients against the original model.

\section{Recap of the Original Model}\label{sec:recap}

\paragraph{Setup.} Let $X \in \{0,1\}^{n \times m}$ be the interaction matrix of
$n$ historical templates and $m$ recipients, where $X_{ij} = 1$ if recipient $j$
opened template $i$. A new template $n+1$ is sent within a deadline $T$ in $b$
batches of $k \ll m$ recipients: the first batch at $t = 0$, the following ones
at intervals of $T/b$, and a final batch at $T$. Let $x(t) \in \{0,1\}^m$ mark
the recipients who have opened template $n+1$ in $[0,t)$, so that $x(0) = \mathbf{0}$,
and let $\bar{x}(t) = x(t) / \max(1, \mathbf{1}^{\top} x(t))$. The goal is to
reach as many of the eventual openers as possible within the sending budget.

\paragraph{Shallow autoencoder.} An SAE with encoder and decoder
$E, D \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d}$ is trained once on $X$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sae}
  \min_{E,D}\; \ell\bigl(X,\, \sigma(X B_{E,D})\bigr), \qquad
  B_{E,D} = E D^{\top} - \operatorname{diag}\bigl([E \odot D]\,\mathbf{1}_d\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\ell$ is the element-wise binary cross-entropy, $\sigma$ the sigmoid, and
the diagonal term sets the diagonal of $B_{E,D}$ to zero, ruling out trivial
self-prediction. The entry $\Sigma_{ij}$ of $\Sigma = \sigma(B_{E,D})$ is the
probability that recipient $j$ opens a template given that only recipient $i$
opened it, i.e., the influence of $i$ on $j$.

\paragraph{Arm score.} Each recipient $j$ is an arm with the score
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score}
  s_j(t) = \alpha\, \varphi_j\, p_j + (1-\alpha)\, f_j(t),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:components}
  \begin{aligned}
    \varphi_j &= \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_{ij}, \qquad
    p_j = \frac{1}{m-1}\sum_{i \neq j} \Sigma_{ji}, \\
    f_j(t) &= \bar{x}(t)^{\top} \Sigma_{:,j},
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\varphi_j$ is the historic open rate of recipient $j$, $p_j$ their general
influence on the other recipients, $f_j(t)$ the confidence that $j$ opens given
the opens of template $n+1$ observed so far, and $\alpha \in [0,1]$ trades off the
two terms. All three quantities are derived from interactions alone, so the
score is purely collaborative; the new template enters it only through the
observed opens $\bar{x}(t)$.

\paragraph{Thompson Sampling.} The score parametrizes a Beta distribution for each
recipient,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ts}
  a_j(t) = G\, s_j(t), \qquad b_j(t) = G\,\bigl(1 - s_j(t)\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $G > 0$ is a confidence modifier: the larger $G$, the narrower the
distribution. In each batch, one value is drawn from every distribution and the
$k$ not yet contacted recipients with the highest draws receive the message.
Since $\Sigma$ is fixed after training, no retraining is needed during active
learning.

\section{Methodology}

\paragraph{Architecture.} We replace the SAE with a deep autoencoder that maps
the open vector $x \in \{0,1\}^m$ of a template to a $d$-dimensional latent code
$z$ and back,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arch}
  \begin{aligned}
    x \to \operatorname{Lin}(m,2d) \to \operatorname{ReLU} \to
    \operatorname{Lin}(2d,d) = z \to \operatorname{Euler}(d,2d) \\
    \to \operatorname{Lin}(2d,m) \to \sigma \to \hat{x}.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first and last layers hold $2dm$ weights each, which is large for
$m = 131{,}918$ recipients compared to the number of training templates. We
address this with standard regularization (e.g., weight decay and dropout) and
expect the disentangling constraint to act as an additional regularizer.

\paragraph{Euler encoding.} The Euler block follows the DAE~\cite{cha2023orthogonality}
and its use in CODAE~\cite{cha2025discovering}. Each latent factor is min-max
normalized over the batch to $\bar{z}_k \in [0,1]$ (with moving statistics at
test time) and then stretched by a learnable affine map
$y_k = \beta_k + \gamma_k \bar{z}_k$, which lets the model choose the arc of the
circle each factor occupies. The result is mapped to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euler}
  u_k = \bigl(\cos y_k,\; \sin y_k\bigr), \qquad k = 1, \dots, d.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Latent factor guidance.} Following CODAE~\cite{cha2025discovering}, we
steer a subset $\mathcal{G} \subseteq \{1,\dots,d\}$ of the normalized latent
factors towards template-level statistics $g^{(k)} \in [0,1]$, which are
computed from the interaction logs of the training templates (e.g., their open
rate or time-to-open statistics) and rescaled to $[0,1]$. The loss is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss}
  \mathcal{L} = \ell(x, \hat{x}) + \beta(e) \sum_{k \in \mathcal{G}}
  \bigl(\bar{z}_k - g^{(k)}\bigr)^2,
\end{equation}
where $\ell$ is the binary cross-entropy as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:sae} and the weight
$\beta(e)$ decreases to zero with the training epoch $e$, so that the guided
factors are first anchored to the statistics and then free to refine them. The
remaining factors are not guided and are left to Euler encoding.
% TODO: decide which statistics and how many factors to guide, and the decay schedule of beta.

\paragraph{Scoring.} The arm score of Eq.~\eqref{eq:score} and Thompson Sampling
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ts} are kept, and only the network-derived quantities change:
$f_j(t)$ is the reconstruction of recipient $j$ from the observed opens,
$f_j(t) = \hat{x}(\bar{x}(t))_j$, and $p_j$ is the mean over $i \neq j$ of the
reconstruction $\hat{x}(e_j)_i$ from the single open of recipient $j$, with $e_j$
the $j$-th unit vector. Both are obtained with a fixed network, so no retraining
is needed during active learning.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} Since each latent factor is meant to capture one
characteristic of a template, its meaning can be inspected by a traversal:
decoding while varying one factor and fixing the others shows how the predicted
opens of the recipients change. Guided factors have a known meaning from their
statistic, and unguided factors are inspected manually.

\section{Experiments}

\paragraph{Protocol.} We follow the offline evaluation of \v{Z}id et
al.~\cite{zid2025active}: the dataset of 131,918 recipients and 160 templates is
split chronologically into training, validation (preceding 5 templates) and test
(last 10 templates) sets, the delays of opens are simulated, the same deadline,
batch size and number of batches are used, and the provider sends to a fraction
$\tau$ of the recipients. Hyperparameters are selected on the validation set and
results are the mean and standard deviation over ten simulations.

\paragraph{Recommendation performance.} We evaluate the framework with
$\mathrm{Recall}@\tau$, the share of the eventual openers among the contacted
recipients, at the low sending fractions $\tau \in \{5, 15, 25, 35\}\%$, where
the savings are largest. As an addition to the original protocol, we report the
Recall-AUC, the area under the $\mathrm{Recall}@\tau$ curve over all
$\tau \in [0, 100]\%$, which summarizes the whole curve in one number. The
original SAE-based MAB is the baseline, re-evaluated under the same protocol so
that the Recall-AUC is available for it, and we ablate Euler encoding and
guidance in the deep autoencoder.

\paragraph{Disentanglement.} The disentanglement is measured on a synthetic
dataset with known template factors using supervised disentanglement
metrics~\cite{carbonneau2022measuring}, and compared with the same deep
autoencoder without Euler encoding. On the original dataset, no ground-truth
factors exist, so we analyze the learned factors manually using latent factor
traversals and, for the guided factors, their relation to the template
statistics.
